# Product positioning

> **Mode:** Explanation
> **Audience:** Readers, contributors, maintainers and future product shapers
> **Outcome:** Understand what CryptoPulse is today, why its evidence-first approach matters, and where its current product boundary ends.

CryptoPulse is an evidence-first, AI-generated crypto market demo and archive. It is not a live market terminal, investment research service, recommendation engine or trading-signal product. Reports and generated content may be inaccurate, incomplete, stale, misleading or hallucinated and must not be relied on for trading, investing or risk decisions.

## Positioning in one sentence

> **CryptoPulse is an open, evidence-first crypto market explorer where public insights are designed to be traceable back to deterministic, repository-owned evidence.**

The shortest expression of the idea is:

> **Don’t trust the AI. Inspect the evidence.**

This is product positioning, not a guarantee that every historical report claim is correct or deterministically derived. The repository deliberately keeps the demo and non-advice boundary visible because generated material can be wrong.

## Why CryptoPulse exists

Many crypto products present a price, chart, summary or AI-generated interpretation without giving the reader a practical way to inspect how the result was produced. CryptoPulse is exploring a different product model: make the evidence path itself part of the user value.

The important question is not only “what does the system say?” It is also “what evidence does the repository actually possess, how was that evidence transformed, what is missing or degraded, and can the result be reproduced?”

That leads to the core product principle: **evidence before intelligence**.

Where a factual transformation can be deterministic, CryptoPulse prefers deterministic repository-owned computation over asking a model to invent, infer or restate the result. AI-generated interpretation can exist, but it should remain visibly subordinate to reviewable evidence rather than becoming the authority for facts that the system can calculate and validate directly.

## What exists today

### AI-generated report archive

CryptoPulse stores report source content as Markdown and publishes a disposable static site through GitHub Pages. The archive makes generated report examples reviewable as repository content rather than leaving them as ephemeral model responses.

The reports remain demonstration material. Their existence in the repository does not establish that every statement is accurate, current or supported.

### Deterministic temporal evidence

The current public product includes one bounded deterministic temporal-evidence surface for:

```text
metric: BTC.price_usd
window: 24 canonical observation-hour slots
```

The evidence is derived from the checked-out trusted repository state under the delivered Phase 13 and Phase 15 contracts. Missing or unavailable slots remain explicit rather than being silently interpolated, backfilled or replaced with older evidence. Provenance and evidence status remain part of the result.

This surface is intentionally narrow. It demonstrates that repository-owned evidence can cross into a public user experience without requiring the reader to trust an opaque model-generated chart.

### Inspectable repository authority

Checked-in source snapshots, accepted analysis records, Markdown reports, contracts, configuration and source assets form the reviewable repository boundary. Generated HTML is derived and disposable rather than an independent source of truth.

This makes CryptoPulse unusual as a demo product: the implementation, evidence, validation rules and delivery history can be inspected together. The aim is not merely reproducible software; it is reproducible reasoning about what evidence the product is entitled to show.

## What makes the product different

CryptoPulse is a **glass-box** approach to AI-assisted market information.

The differentiation is not “we use AI.” AI is commonplace. The differentiation is that the product tries to minimise the amount of trust a reader must place in an opaque AI response.

A CryptoPulse surface should increasingly be able to answer questions such as:

- What repository-owned evidence produced this value?
- Which exact observation or record was used?
- Was evidence missing, degraded or ambiguous?
- Was the result calculated deterministically or generated by a model?
- Can the same repository state reproduce the same result?

When the evidence cannot support a claim, the preferred behaviour is to expose that limitation rather than manufacture a smoother story.

## What CryptoPulse is not today

CryptoPulse should not currently be presented as:

- a live CoinMarketCap- or TradingView-style market terminal;
- a broad multi-asset dashboard;
- an investment research service;
- a prediction or forecasting product;
- a recommendation or trading-signal provider;
- a personalised alerting service;
- a portfolio-management product;
- a source of guaranteed accurate, current or profitable market conclusions.

The deterministic publication control plane delivered in Phase 14 also remains at its safe inert boundary. Live pilot publication, recurring deterministic publication and the deferred production stale-base proof are not part of the current product claim.

## Product principles

### Evidence before intelligence

Use deterministic, reviewable transformations wherever the repository already has sufficient evidence. Do not ask a model to become the authority for a fact that can be computed directly.

### Provenance is part of the product

A result becomes more useful when a reader or reviewer can trace it to immutable evidence and understand the transformation that produced it.

### Missing evidence is information

Gaps, ambiguity, degradation and validation failure should remain visible. A clean-looking chart is not more trustworthy if it hides uncertainty.

### AI must identify itself

Readers should understand that CryptoPulse contains AI-generated demonstration content before they rely on a market claim. The demo, possible-inaccuracy and non-advice boundaries are product requirements, not footer boilerplate.

### Generated output is disposable

The repository-owned evidence and reviewed source state are the authority. Generated HTML can be rebuilt and should not become an independent source of truth.

### Fail closed rather than overclaim

When a decision-critical property has not actually been proved, record the gap and stop. The product should prefer an explicit limitation to unsupported confidence.

## Reusable positioning language

### Recommended one-line description

> CryptoPulse is an open, evidence-first crypto market explorer where public insights are designed to be traceable back to deterministic, repository-owned evidence.

### Short pitch

CryptoPulse turns crypto market data into inspectable public evidence. Instead of asking readers to trust an opaque AI answer, it keeps the source evidence, deterministic transformations, gaps and provenance reviewable in the repository. AI-generated reports remain clearly labelled demonstration content and are subordinate to the evidence rather than treated as investment authority.

### Candidate taglines

> **Don’t trust the AI. Inspect the evidence.**

> **Crypto without the black box.**

These are positioning options, not new product guarantees.

## Boundary for future shaping

This document records the product proposition that exists after Phase 15. It does not select Phase 16, activate deferred Phase 14 publication work or authorise any follow-on implementation.

Future product shaping should ask whether a proposed capability makes CryptoPulse materially more useful to a reader **without weakening the evidence-first boundary**. A technically interesting addition is not automatically a good next phase. The next product decision should be driven by user value and trustworthy evidence together.
